# Contributor Covenant Code of Conduct

## Belgian Society of Paediatric Haematology and Oncology (`BelgianSPHO`) Open-Source Community

---

## Our Pledge

We as members, clinicians, researchers, contributors, and leaders of the **Belgian Society of Paediatric Haematology and Oncology (BSPHO)** open-source ecosystem pledge to make participation in our community a harassment-free, psychologically safe, and deeply collaborative experience for everyone—regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of clinical or technical experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and clinically responsible community dedicated to improving care for children and adolescents with cancer and blood disorders across Belgium and beyond.

---

## Our Interdisciplinary Ethos & Clinical Principles

The `BelgianSPHO` community brings together a unique interdisciplinary coalition developed through the **Hacking4Good / Solve with Google x BSPHO Pediatric Oncology Hackathon (2026)** and ongoing clinical AI initiatives across four foundational clinical missions:

- **UC1 — Protocol Search Assistant (`OncoProtocol` / Red Zone)**
- **UC2 — Pediatric Prescriber (`PediDose` / Green Zone)**
- **UC3 — CINV Management (`AImesis` / Black Zone)**
- **UC4 — Belgian Pediatric Clinical Trial ICF (`AutoICF` / Blue Zone)**

Our contributors include **pediatric oncologists, hematologists, oncology nurses, clinical trial coordinators, hospital pharmacists, bioinformaticians, AI and cloud engineers, regulatory specialists, and patient/family advocates**. Because our software directly touches pediatric oncology workflows, we uphold five additional domain commitments alongside the Contributor Covenant v2.1:

1. **Mutual Respect Across Clinical & Technical Domains**: Zero tolerance for academic, clinical, or technical elitism. A pediatric nurse flagging a bedside workflow friction point, a pharmacist questioning a body-surface-area (BSA) rounding rule, a patient advocate reviewing Informed Consent Form (ICF) readability, and an engineer debugging a retrieval pipeline all hold equal standing in technical and clinical discussions.
2. **Psychological Safety in Raising Clinical Safety Concerns**: Every contributor is empowered—and expected—to halt a pull request or raise a `clinical-safety` concern when an edge case, hallucination risk, dosing ambiguity, or regulatory gap is identified. Raising a patient safety concern in good faith will always be celebrated, never penalized or dismissed.
3. **Patient Dignity & Strict Privacy Protection (Zero PHI/PII)**: All discourse, issue reports, datasets, prompts, and code contributions must honor the dignity of pediatric patients and their families. **Protected Health Information (PHI) and Personally Identifiable Information (PII)—including Belgian National Register Numbers (`rijksregisternummer` / `NISS`), Medical Record Numbers (MRNs), patient names, or identifiable clinical narratives—must never be shared, committed, or uploaded** anywhere in `BelgianSPHO` repositories.
4. **Mandatory Clinical Oversight ("Advisory Only")**: Contributors must never represent experimental code or AI outputs in this organization as autonomous medical devices or replacements for qualified clinical judgment. All clinical decision-support artifacts must maintain explicit `"Advisory Only — Requires Clinician Sign-Off"` framing.
5. **Honest Evaluation & Scientific Integrity**: In hackathons, benchmarks, and clinical validation suites, contributors must compete and collaborate with complete scientific honesty. Adversarial prompt injection, hidden evaluation instructions, benchmark gaming, or sabotage of other teams' clinical prototypes violates the trust at the heart of pediatric oncology research.

## Our Standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment for our community include:

* Demonstrating empathy, humility, and kindness toward other people across clinical, scientific, and engineering backgrounds.
* Respecting differing viewpoints, institutional practices across Belgian pediatric oncology centers (UZ Gent, Cliniques Universitaires Saint-Luc, UZA, UZ Leuven, HUDERF, CHU Liège, UZ Brussel, and partner hospitals), and interdisciplinary experiences.
* Giving and gracefully accepting constructive clinical and technical feedback.
* Accepting responsibility and apologizing to those affected by our mistakes, and learning from the experience—especially when addressing clinical edge cases or software bugs.
* Focusing on what is best not just for us as individuals or hackathon teams, but for pediatric oncology patients, families, care teams, and the broader `BelgianSPHO` community.
* Using clear, accessible language and willingly explaining domain-specific acronyms (whether clinical protocols like `ALLTogether1` and `MASCC/POGO`, or technical concepts like RAG, ADK, and CI/CD) so all contributors can participate fully.

Examples of unacceptable behavior include:

* The use of sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind.
* Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal, institutional, or political attacks.
* Dismissive, condescending, or elitist behavior toward contributors from different professional disciplines (e.g., dismissing non-coders' clinical feedback or belittling non-clinicians' questions about medical protocols).
* Public or private harassment, intimidation, or retaliation against anyone who reports a clinical safety flaw, code vulnerability, or Code of Conduct violation.
* **Committing, posting, or transmitting real patient data (PHI/PII), confidential hospital records, or unredacted clinical identifiers** in issues, pull requests, commit histories, or discussion threads.
* Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email address, without their explicit permission.
* **Evaluation manipulation or sabotage**, including embedding hidden prompt-injection instructions, invisible Unicode characters, or adversarial payloads aimed at automated grading systems, clinical evaluators, or fellow contributors.
* Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional medical, scientific, and open-source software setting.

## Enforcement Responsibilities

Community leaders—including the **BSPHO Board & Secretariat**, **Clinical Use Case Leads**, and **Technical Maintainers**—are responsible for clarifying and enforcing our standards of acceptable behavior and will take appropriate and fair corrective action in response to any behavior that they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive, clinically unsafe, or harmful.

Community leaders have the right and responsibility to remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, wiki edits, issues, and other contributions that are not aligned to this Code of Conduct (including immediate redaction and history purge of any suspected PHI/PII or leaked credentials), and will communicate reasons for moderation decisions when appropriate.

---

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all `BelgianSPHO` community spaces—including GitHub repositories, issues, pull requests, code reviews, discussions, documentation, and hackathon collaboration channels—and also applies when an individual is officially representing the community in public spaces, clinical conferences, or partner events (such as **Hacking4Good / Solve with Google x BSPHO**). Examples of representing our community include using an official `@bspho.be` email address, posting via an official social media account, or acting as an appointed representative at an online or offline event.

---

## Enforcement & Reporting

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behavior—as well as urgent community integrity or privacy concerns—may be reported to the **BSPHO Secretariat** responsible for enforcement at:

* **Email**: [`secretary@bspho.be`](mailto:secretary@bspho.be)
* **Organization Website**: [https://bspho.be/](https://bspho.be/)

All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly by the BSPHO Secretariat and designated governance leads.

All community leaders are obligated to respect the privacy and security of the reporter of any incident. If a report involves a specific Clinical Use Case Lead or Technical Maintainer, that individual will be recused from the review and enforcement process.

## Enforcement Guidelines

Community leaders will follow these Community Impact Guidelines in determining the consequences for any action they deem in violation of this Code of Conduct:

### 1. Correction
* **Community Impact**: Use of inappropriate language or other behavior deemed unprofessional or unwelcome in the community (such as dismissive interdisciplinary remarks or minor etiquette breaches).
* **Consequence**: A private, written warning from community leaders, providing clarity around the nature of the violation and an explanation of why the behavior was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.

### 2. Warning
* **Community Impact**: A violation through a single incident or series of actions (such as repeated disregard for clinical safety review processes, persistent hostility in PR reviews, or negligent handling of synthetic vs. real data boundaries).
* **Consequence**: A warning with consequences for continued behavior. No interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, for a specified period of time. This includes avoiding interactions in community spaces as well as external channels like social media. Violating these terms may lead to a temporary or permanent ban.

### 3. Temporary Ban
* **Community Impact**: A serious violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior, retaliatory conduct against someone raising a clinical safety concern, or intentional attempts to bypass evaluation integrity rules.
* **Consequence**: A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public communication with the community for a specified period of time. No public or private interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, is allowed during this period. Violating these terms may lead to a permanent ban.

### 4. Permanent Ban
* **Community Impact**: Demonstrating a pattern of violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior, harassment of an individual, aggression toward or disparagement of classes of individuals, deliberate exposure of patient PHI/PII, or malicious sabotage of clinical decision-support repositories.
* **Consequence**: A permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within the `BelgianSPHO` community and disqualification from affiliated hackathon or research programs.
